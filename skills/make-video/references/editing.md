# Editorial decisions

## Analyze the target and material

Inspect the reference's relevant interval, aspect, opening hold, cut times, exceptional shot lengths, transitions, typography, motion, and color. Its music may be rearranged: establish its relationship to the supplied song before translating cut timestamps. If inaccessible, identify what could not be inspected and use the user's description rather than inventing observations.

Make a contact sheet and shot log with source IDs. Time-of-day storytelling uses visible light and setting, supported by metadata. Footage from several dates can form a visual day without being described as one chronological day. If morning material is absent, use the earliest-looking daylight and identify the limitation. A requested all-clips edit must account for each asset; explain genuine exclusions.

Alternate scale, subject, movement, and location with purpose. Separate near-duplicate train or skyline shots unless adjacency develops an action. A strong shot can deserve a longer hold; including more footage does not require equal lengths. Enter after camera settling, retain the action's payoff, and leave before energy falls. “Cut later” may mean later source out-points, musical boundaries, or both: inspect the actual mismatch.

## Music drives structure

Honor a requested song start at zero and preserve its buildup. Listen where tools permit; waveform, onset, and energy analysis support phrase decisions but do not establish listening. Mark phrase starts, fills, vocal entrances, and candidate endings. Cutting every drum hit produces a different result from a relaxed montage. Roughly three-second holds suited one credits-inspired project; choose current pacing from the reference and song.

Choose a musical endpoint before stretching a timeline to fit every asset. Prefer a resolved phrase or deliberate release. If a fade works best, align and describe it as an editorial fade rather than asserting natural harmonic resolution. Picture and audio fades may differ. Keep a clean soundtrack, known offset, and deliberate gain; duplicated stereo tracks alter level and image.

## Still photographs and graphics

Respect EXIF orientation and ICC profiles before framing. Protect faces, landmarks, and text. Contain when the full composition matters; cover when cropping improves it and the target permits. Portraits in landscape need deliberate framing, not automatic face removal.

Use restrained zoom/pan with enough resolution for the largest crop. Let motion reveal a relationship or lead into the next image. Mix static holds and movement. Oversample moving crops or use subpixel transforms to avoid pixel stepping; review playback. The bundled renderer provides centered zooms; use an editor/compositor for focal-point paths, masks, complex transitions, or parallax.

Keep titles readable at delivery size, with safe margins and sufficient hold. Place them over supportive compositions. Use supplied or appropriately licensed fonts/media and retain external asset provenance.

## Revisions

Revise the user's specific concern. “Less saturated and sharp” needs gentler color/detail treatment, not lower bitrate. “More trains” does not imply adjacent train shots. Inspect newly added media and integrate it into the story rather than automatically appending it. Preserve earlier deliveries and version the manifest/project.
